Sleep and Shift Work

How to sleep well when you work nights, the best times to nap, and how to protect your health on a rotating roster.

Shift work, especially night shifts, fights the body clock, because the clock is built to sleep at night and be awake in the day. Night workers sleep less, sleep worse, and carry higher risks of accidents and health problems. The risks can be reduced, though, with planning around light, naps and sleep timing. This guide covers the practical steps that help most.

Why night work is hard on sleep

The body clock promotes sleep at night and wakefulness in the day, driven by light and by the hormone melatonin. Working at night means staying awake when the clock says sleep, and sleeping in the day when the clock says wake. Daytime sleep is shorter, lighter and more broken, typically by one to two hours, because daylight, noise and a body clock that is promoting wakefulness all interfere. Rotating shifts are hardest of all, because the clock never settles on one pattern.

Sleeping after a night shift

Sleep as soon as you can after the shift, rather than staying up to run errands. Wear dark sunglasses on the way home, because morning light tells the clock to wake up and makes it harder to fall asleep. Make the bedroom dark with blackout curtains, cool, and quiet, using earplugs or white noise if needed. Tell family and housemates your sleep hours, and put your phone on silent. Aim for a single block of sleep of 7 to 8 hours where possible, rather than two short blocks.

Naps before and during shifts

A nap of 90 minutes before a night shift, in the late afternoon or early evening, reduces tiredness during the shift. If your workplace allows it, a short nap of 20 minutes during the shift, taken before the middle of the night, also helps, though it is not always possible. Avoid napping in the last few hours of a shift, because the grogginess afterwards can be dangerous for safety-critical work. Caffeine early in the shift helps alertness, but avoid it in the last four hours, so it does not prevent sleep afterwards.

Light and food on nights

Bright light during the first half of a night shift helps alertness, and dim light in the last hours helps you wind down for sleep. Eat a main meal before the shift, a light meal in the middle, and a small snack rather than a heavy meal in the early morning, because digestion is slower at night and heavy food causes discomfort. Avoid alcohol after a shift: it may help you fall asleep, but it fragments sleep and makes it less restorative.

Long-term health on shifts

Shift work is linked to higher risks of heart disease, diabetes and some other conditions, partly through short sleep and partly through disrupted eating and activity. The best defences are the basics: keep a regular sleep pattern on days off, exercise regularly, eat regular meals, and attend health checks. If you work rotating shifts, a forward rotation, days to evenings to nights, is easier on the body than a backward one. If sleep problems persist, talk to your doctor, because there are treatments and workplace adjustments that can help.

A night shift, planned hour by hour

Take a night shift from 10 p.m. to 6 a.m. Nap for 90 minutes in the late afternoon, from about 4:30 to 6 p.m., then eat a main meal before the shift. During the shift, drink coffee in the first half and switch to water after 2 a.m. Eat a light meal around midnight and a small snack at 4 a.m. Wear dark sunglasses on the way home, go straight to bed by 7 a.m., and sleep until about 3 p.m. in a dark, cool, quiet room. The plan is built around light: bright light early in the shift for alertness, darkness afterwards for sleep, and sunglasses to block the morning light that would otherwise tell the clock to wake up.

Tip: Sleep as soon as you can after a night shift, and wear dark sunglasses on the way home — morning light tells the clock to wake up and makes falling asleep harder.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much sleep do night shift workers get?

Usually 1 to 2 hours less than day workers, and the sleep is lighter and more broken, because daylight and the body clock interfere.

Should I nap before a night shift?

Yes. A 90-minute nap in the late afternoon or early evening reduces tiredness during the shift.

Does caffeine help on night shifts?

Yes, early in the shift. Avoid it in the last four hours, so it does not prevent sleep afterwards.
