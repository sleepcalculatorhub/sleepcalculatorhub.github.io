The Power Nap Guide

How to take a 10 to 20 minute nap that refreshes you, when to nap, and the mistakes that leave you groggier.

A power nap is a short sleep of 10 to 20 minutes, taken in the early afternoon, that refreshes alertness without the grogginess of a longer sleep. It is one of the most researched habits in sleep science, and it works because it stays in light sleep and ends before deep sleep begins. This guide explains how to take one well, when to take it, and what to avoid.

Why 20 minutes is the sweet spot

A nap of 10 to 20 minutes stays in the light stages of sleep, so you wake easily and feel refreshed within a few minutes. A nap of 30 minutes or more can drop you into deep sleep, and waking from deep sleep causes sleep inertia: grogginess, confusion and a heavy feeling that can last half an hour. That is why a 20-minute nap feels better than a 45-minute one, even though it is shorter. The exception is a 90-minute nap, which covers a full cycle and ends in light sleep again, so it also feels good, but it takes much longer.

The best time to nap

The best window for a nap is early afternoon, roughly between 1 p.m. and 3 p.m., when the body naturally dips in alertness after lunch. This dip is part of the body clock, not a sign of laziness, and a short nap fits it well. Napping after about 3 p.m. can make it harder to fall asleep at night, because it reduces the sleep pressure that builds up during the day. If you work nights, the best time is before your shift, not during it, unless your workplace allows a short break.

How to take a power nap

Find a quiet, dark, comfortable place, set an alarm for 25 minutes, which allows about 5 minutes to fall asleep and 20 minutes of sleep, and lie down. A short coffee before the nap, sometimes called a coffee nap, can help: caffeine takes about 20 minutes to work, so it kicks in just as you wake. Do not use your phone in bed, and do not try to force sleep; even resting quietly with your eyes closed for 20 minutes is refreshing. Get up when the alarm rings, step into light, and move around for a minute.

Who benefits most

Power naps help anyone who is short of sleep, but they help shift workers, new parents, and people with long commutes most of all. They improve alertness, mood and performance for a few hours afterwards, and they are a better response to afternoon tiredness than a sugary snack or a large coffee. They do not replace a full night's sleep, and relying on naps instead of fixing a short night is not a good long-term plan, but as a supplement they are one of the most effective tools available.

When naps do not help

Naps can make insomnia worse, because they reduce the sleep pressure that helps you fall asleep at night. If you have trouble falling asleep at night, avoid naps, or keep them very short and early. Naps also do not help if you are unwell, and persistent daytime sleepiness despite a full night's sleep should be discussed with a doctor, because it can signal a sleep disorder. A nap is a tool for a tired day, not a treatment for a sleep problem.

A coffee nap, step by step

A coffee nap combines caffeine and a short sleep. Drink a cup of coffee, quickly, then lie down and set an alarm for 25 minutes. The caffeine takes about 20 minutes to reach the brain, so it kicks in just as you wake, and the short sleep clears some of the adenosine that causes sleepiness. The result is more alertness than either the coffee or the nap alone. The steps matter: drink the coffee fast, so the timing works, nap immediately, and get up when the alarm rings, stepping into light. Do not try it late in the day, because the caffeine will disturb the night, and do not lengthen the nap, because waking from deep sleep undoes the benefit.

Tip: A 20-minute nap feels better than a 45-minute one, because it ends before deep sleep begins. The exception is a 90-minute nap, which covers a full cycle.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long should a power nap be?

10 to 20 minutes. That stays in light sleep, so you wake refreshed instead of groggy.

When is the best time to nap?

Early afternoon, roughly 1 to 3 p.m., when the body naturally dips in alertness. Avoid napping after about 3 p.m.

What is a coffee nap?

Drink a coffee, then nap for 20 minutes. The caffeine takes about 20 minutes to work, so it kicks in just as you wake.
