The Best Bedtime for an Early Riser

Bedtimes for 5 a.m., 5:30 a.m. and 6 a.m. starts, and how to make early mornings work without feeling exhausted.

Waking up early is easy to admire and hard to do well, because it only works if bedtime moves earlier by the same amount. An early alarm without an early bedtime is simply a short night, and a short night repeated for weeks leaves you tired, irritable and less able to think clearly. This guide gives the bedtimes for common early starts and the habits that make early rising sustainable.

Bedtimes for a 5 a.m. start

To wake at 5 a.m. with 7.5 hours of sleep, five cycles, you need to be asleep by 9:30 p.m., which means going to bed around 9:15 p.m. if you take 15 minutes to fall asleep. For 9 hours, six cycles, you need to be asleep by 8 p.m., in bed by 7:45 p.m. Those times feel early, and they are, but they are the price of a 5 a.m. start. A 6-hour night, four cycles, means asleep by 11 p.m., but that is not enough for most people night after night, and it will catch up with you within days.

Bedtimes for 5:30 and 6 a.m. starts

For a 5:30 a.m. start, five cycles means asleep by 10 p.m., in bed by 9:45 p.m. Six cycles means asleep by 8:30 p.m. For a 6 a.m. start, five cycles means asleep by 10:30 p.m., in bed by 10:15 p.m., and six cycles means asleep by 9 p.m. These are the times the calculator gives, and they are worth writing down, because the common mistake is to keep a 11 p.m. bedtime and simply set an earlier alarm. The maths does not allow that: the sleep has to come from somewhere.

Shifting your schedule earlier

Moving your bedtime earlier by an hour overnight rarely works. Shift it by 15 minutes every two or three nights, moving both bedtime and wake time earlier together, until you reach the target. Morning light helps more than anything: get bright light within an hour of waking, ideally daylight, because it resets the body clock. Evening light does the opposite, so dim the lights and avoid bright screens in the last hour before bed. Exercise in the morning rather than late evening also helps the shift.

The social cost of early rising

An early bedtime has a real social cost: evening events, late dinners and television all compete with it. It helps to decide in advance which evenings are exceptions and which are not, rather than letting every evening drift late. Tell friends and family that you keep an early schedule, and plan social time for mornings or weekends when you can. An early riser who protects the bedtime most nights and relaxes it once or twice a week does far better than one who is early on weekdays and chaotic at weekends.

When early rising is not working

If you go to bed at the calculated time and still feel exhausted, the problem may not be the schedule. Check the basics: a dark, cool, quiet room, no alcohol, no late caffeine, and no screens in bed. If tiredness persists for weeks despite a full night's sleep, see a doctor, because it can signal a sleep disorder such as sleep apnoea, or another health issue. Early rising should leave you feeling clear and energetic, not permanently tired, and persistent exhaustion is a sign to look deeper.

A week of early rising, planned

Take a person moving from a 7:30 a.m. wake time to a 5:30 a.m. one. They shift both bedtime and wake time earlier by 15 minutes every two nights: 7:15 a.m., then 7:00, 6:45, 6:30, 6:15, 6:00, 5:45, and finally 5:30, over about two weeks. Each morning, they get bright light within an hour of waking, ideally daylight outdoors. Each evening, they dim the lights an hour before bed and avoid screens. By the end of the second week, the 5:30 a.m. start feels normal, because the clock has moved gradually rather than being forced. The plan is slow, and that is why it works: the body clock moves about an hour a day at most, and a gradual shift respects that limit.

Tip: A 5 a.m. start needs a 9:30 p.m. sleep time for 7.5 hours — the sleep has to come from somewhere, and an early alarm without an early bedtime is just a short night.

Frequently Asked Questions

What time should I go to bed if I wake at 6 a.m.?

For 7.5 hours, be asleep by 10:30 p.m. For 9 hours, be asleep by 9 p.m. Add your time to fall asleep on top.

How do I shift my schedule earlier?

Move bedtime and wake time earlier by 15 minutes every two or three nights, and get bright light within an hour of waking.

Is waking at 5 a.m. healthy?

It can be, if bedtime moves earlier to match. An early alarm without an early bedtime is simply a short night.
